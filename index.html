Why this car needs the deep read. Hyundai reacquired it in June 2026 after two battery repairs in five months, and it is still open on the fire recall 26V432. So the two things you are hunting for are a weak 12V / ICCU charging fault and any stored fault codes.

The small square beside each step turns green once you have read it, amber if something is worth a second look, and red if it is a flag. A red box below means renegotiate or walk.

1

Pair the adapter at home, not in their lot

Do this tonight on your own car. Ten minutes here saves twenty in a parking lot.

On the iPhoneApp Store → install IONIQ 5 Companion
  • Only one app can hold a BLE adapter at a time. Close Car Scanner and anything else that talks to the dongle.
  • The adapter must be Bluetooth Low Energy. Wi-Fi adapters and classic-Bluetooth units will never appear.
  • If your adapter does not show in the list, turn off the name filter as the footer text says.
Write it down. If the name is missing from the list on Saturday, you will know it is the phone, not the car.
2

Plug in, start the car, open the Dashboard

The port is a 16-pin trapezoid under the dash by the steering base.

Order of operationsPort → press Start to READY → open app → Dashboard tab
  • The port sits on the driver side lower dash bezel, near the base of the steering column. Same 16-pin SAE J1962 connector every car since 1996 uses.
  • Press the start button so the car is in READY, not accessory. Most of the pack and 12V signals do not publish in accessory mode.
  • Tap Dashboard in the bottom tab bar. It is the leftmost of the four tabs.
No green dot means every number below is stale. Re-seat the dongle and try again before reading anything.
3

Confirm it is the right car

The VIN and the odometer are the two fields that catch a mix-up.

Tap pathDashboard → Overview card, top of screen
  • Read the VIN strip under the gauge. It must be KM8KNDAF6PU198280, character for character.
  • Odometer should be within a hundred miles of 40,770. A number far off means the cluster or the ECU was swapped, or the listing is not this car.
  • Model IONIQ 5, Model Year 2023, Variant should say AWD, not RWD.

Also read the dash cluster and compare. The two should agree.
4

Pack level, energy left, isolation

SoC and kWh available together give you the real usable capacity, which is your best stand-in for state of health.

Tap pathDashboard → SoC ring, then the kWh available line under it
  • Write down State of Charge and kWh available at the same moment. They are only useful as a pair.
  • The 2023 Long Range AWD pack is 77.4 kWh nominal, 74.0 kWh usable. Divide kWh available by the SoC fraction and you get the pack's current usable capacity.
  • Isolation Resistance sits next to Gear. Higher is better. Tap the small blue info dot on that card for the app's own guidance.
  • Watch Polling headroom at the bottom of the card. If it collapses toward zero the adapter is struggling and reads will be slow or stale.

5

Cell voltages and the 192-cell grid

Scroll the Overview card. Cell balance is the read that catches a pack on its way out.

Tap pathDashboard → scroll past Overview → Cell Voltages
  • Battery Configuration should read 192 series x 2 parallel. That confirms the 77.4 kWh Long Range pack, not the 58 kWh Standard Range.
  • Read Min, Avg, Max and Delta. Delta is the only one that decides anything.
  • The grid's own legend is the threshold: green under 30 mV, yellow 30 to 100 mV, red over 100 mV. Count any yellow or red cells.
mV
Under 30 is green, 30 to 100 is watch, over 100 is a problem. Do not read this at a very low or very high SoC, where the spread naturally widens.

6

Module temperatures

Keep scrolling. Sixteen modules, and the spread between them matters more than the absolute numbers.

Tap pathDashboard → scroll → Battery · Temperatures
  • Read Min, Avg, Max, Delta. A pack that has been sitting should show a small spread.
  • A single module far off the others points at a coolant or module fault, which is exactly the family of problem that ends in a buyback.
  • Battery Warmer is informational. On a warm October day it should be idle.
°F
Under 10 is tight, 10 to 20 is watch, over 20 means something is not cooling.

7

12V and LDC, the actual buyback cause

This is the one. Two battery repairs and a charging-system check, then Hyundai took the car back.

Tap pathHistory tab → Signals → scroll to 12V & LDC
  • You can also get a live 12V read from the Dashboard 12V row. The History view is the one that keeps a record.
  • With the car in READY, the DC-DC converter should be holding the 12V system around 13.5 to 14.7 V and pushing its state of charge up.
  • The ICCU failure mode shows up here first: 12V voltage sagging toward or below 12.4 V with the car on, or a state of charge stuck low and not climbing.
  • Note the sign convention on current. Positive is charging the 12V, negative is draining it.
V
Car on: 13.5 to 14.7 is right. Below 12.4 with the car running is the ICCU symptom.
%
Above 80 is healthy, 50 to 80 is watch, under 50 on a running car is a flag.
A
Read it twice, a minute apart. A steady negative while the car runs means something is draining the 12V.

Before you leave the 12V screen, have them put the car on a Level 2 charger and watch these same three fields for ten minutes. AC charging is the path the ICCU actually handles, so it is the test that reproduces the failure. DC fast charging bypasses it and will look fine.

8

ICCU Information

The charging control unit's own identification. This is the part that was repaired, and the part the recall replaces.

Tap pathInspect tab → ICCU Information
  • Tap Inspect in the bottom tab bar, then the green ICCU Information card.
  • Copy the identification details exactly as shown. Part numbers and software versions are what a Hyundai service advisor can check against the recall.
  • Take a screenshot of this screen. It is the single most useful thing to hand a service department if the car misbehaves later.
9

Run the Enhanced DTC Scan

Every module the car exposes, every stored fault code. This is the read that can end the deal, or save it.

Tap pathInspect → Enhanced DTC Scan → run, then wait it out
  • Let it finish. Interrupting a multi-module scan is how you get a false clean report.
  • P0C17 on the drive motor controller is benign. It is a permanent self-test artifact that shows up on virtually every Hyundai EV, and the app prints an explanation when it sees it. Do not let it spook you.
  • Anything else stored on the ICCU, the onboard charger, the BMS or the drive units is the finding you came for. Screenshot the list.
  • Read the codes, then ask them to clear nothing. You want the history intact for the service department.
modules
A low count means the scan was cut short or the adapter dropped. Re-run it.
Write "none" if the list is empty. If it is only P0C17, write that.
10

Quick J1979 pass, as a cross-check

Thirty seconds, and it confirms the adapter is talking the standard protocol properly.

Tap pathInspect → J1979 Diagnostic Crawl
  • This is the universal OBD-II layer: vehicle ID, calibration data and standard stored codes.
  • Use it as a sanity check that the adapter and the app agree with each other. If the quick pass and the thorough scan disagree, trust the thorough one and re-run.
11

Watch a real AC charging session

Ten minutes on their Level 2 charger. This is the test that reproduces the fault the car was bought back for.

Tap pathPlug in AC → History → Sessions → the live session
  • Ask them to plug the car into their Level 2 AC charger and let it run ten minutes while you watch.
  • Confirm Charge Type reads AC. A DC session proves nothing here, because DC bypasses the onboard charger entirely.
  • Watch the session hold steady. A session that faults, restarts, or throttles hard on AC is the ICCU telling on itself.
  • Note the EVSE max voltage and current against what the car actually pulls. A car that accepts a fraction of what the EVSE offers is limiting itself.
  • If they will not let you charge, write that down too. It is information.
12

Battery odometer, then a slow drive

Lifetime energy accounting, and the seat-of-the-pants checks that no scan can do.

Tap pathDashboard → scroll to Battery Odometer
  • Efficiency is Charged divided by Discharged. Around 90 to 95 percent is normal for a pack with losses.
  • Because the pack was replaced in January 2026, these lifetime counters may have reset with it. Low totals are expected and are not a fault.
  • Then drive it. Confirm no loss of drive power under load, which is the driveshaft recall 24V065, and that regen behaves on lift-off.
  • Check the dash for any warning lamp that has been cleared rather than fixed. The scan tells you stored codes; the lamp tells you what the car thinks right now.
