1

Pair the adapter at home, tonight

Ten minutes on your own car saves twenty in their lot, and you cannot debug Bluetooth with a salesperson waiting.

On the iPhoneApp Store → install IONIQ 5 Companion, then Scan on the adapter row
  • It must be Bluetooth Low Energy. Wi-Fi and classic-Bluetooth adapters never appear, no matter what you do.
  • Only one app can hold the adapter at a time. Close Car Scanner and anything else that talks to the dongle.
If this is No at home, it will not be Yes on Saturday.
2

Plug in, start the car, open the Dashboard

The port is the 16-pin trapezoid on the driver side lower dash bezel, by the base of the steering column.

Order of operationsPort → press Start to READY → open app → Dashboard tab
  • Press the start button so the car is in READY, not accessory. Most pack and 12V signals do not publish in accessory mode, so an accessory reading is a wasted reading.
No green dot means every number below is stale. Re-seat the dongle before reading anything.

Confirm it is the right car

Two facts, and they are the guard against the app being bound to something else.

Tap pathDashboard → the Overview card at the top

  • Odometer should be within a hundred miles of 40,770. Compare against the dash cluster as well.
  • Ignore the Variant line. It is cosmetic here, and the VIN already fixes what the car is.

4

Pack level, energy left, isolation

Three recorded numbers, no grade. There is no published state-of-health figure in this app, and I am not going to invent one.

Tap pathDashboard → the SoC ring, then the kWh available line beneath it
  • The 2023 Long Range AWD pack is 77.4 kWh nominal, 74.0 kWh usable. SoC and kWh available together let me sanity-check that, and nothing more.
  • The usual shortcut is to divide kWh available by the SoC fraction and call the result a state of health. Do not trust that here. If the app derives its energy figure from a fixed pack constant, the division just returns the constant and tells you nothing. Record both numbers and I will read them.
  • This pack was replaced in January 2026 at 35,396 miles, so it is five thousand miles old on a 40,770-mile car.
  • Isolation Resistance is the HV insulation resistance, in kΩ. Higher is better. Tap the info dot on that card for the app's own guidance, since it publishes no colour legend for it.
  • Watch Polling headroom at the foot of the card. If it collapses toward zero the adapter is struggling and the reads are unreliable.

5

Cell delta and the 192-cell grid

The one health read in this whole app that publishes its own thresholds, so it is the one worth grading.

Tap pathDashboard → scroll past Overview → Cell Voltages
  • Battery Configuration should read 192 series x 2 parallel. That is the 77.4 kWh Long Range pack. If it reads anything smaller, stop and tell me, because the car is not what the listing says.
  • Read Delta and nothing else from the Min, Avg, Max row. Delta is the only one that decides anything, and the other two are typing.
  • The grid's legend is the threshold: green under 30 mV, yellow 30 to 100, red over 100. Count any that are not green.
  • Glance at the Module Temperatures grid a scroll further down, and note any single module that sits well off the others. Nothing to type. On a parked car the whole pack is usually at ambient, so this is a weak test and I am not scoring it.
  • Keep clear of the extremes of SoC for this read. Cell spread widens naturally at a nearly full or nearly empty pack.

6

The ICCU test: 12V at rest, then on AC

The buyback cause, and the only step here that reproduces a fault on purpose. Read the 12V, then have them plug it in.

Tap pathHistory → Signals → 12V & LDC. Then plug in AC and watch History → Sessions
  • With the car in READY, the DC-DC converter should hold the 12V system at roughly 13.5 to 14.7 V and push its state of charge up. Those two are real electrical facts, so they are graded.
  • The ICCU failure shows here first: voltage sagging toward or below 12.4 V with the car running, or a state of charge that sits low and does not climb while you watch it.
  • Then have them put it on their Level 2 AC charger for ten minutes. Confirm Charge Type reads AC. A session that faults, restarts, or throttles hard on AC is the onboard charger telling on itself. DC bypasses the onboard charger completely and will look fine, which proves nothing.
  • Read the current sign convention as positive charging the 12V, negative draining it. No box for it, it is not a decider.
V
Graded: 13.5 and up green, 12.4 to 13.5 amber, below 12.4 red.
%
Read twice, a minute apart. Graded: 80 and up green, 50 to 80 amber, below 50 red. It should be climbing while the car runs, which matters more than the number.
A refusal or a missing charger is amber, not green. Write it down, it is information about the seller.

Do not leave the lot without this. If they will not let the car sit on a charger for ten minutes, that refusal is the finding, and it is worth more than anything the scanner told you.

7

Enhanced DTC Scan, the highest-value read here

Every module the car exposes, every stored fault code. This is the one that can end the deal.

Tap pathInspect → Enhanced DTC Scan → let it finish
  • Let it run to the end. Interrupting a multi-module scan is how you get a false clean report.
  • P0C17 on the drive motor controller is benign. A permanent self-test artifact that shows up on virtually every Hyundai EV, and the app prints its own explanation when it sees it. It is the one code that does not count against the car.
  • Anything else on the ICCU, onboard charger, BMS or drive units is what you came for. Screenshot the list.
  • Also tap ICCU Information once and screenshot it. Do not retype part numbers into your phone in a parking lot. I will read them off the screenshot and check them against the recall.
  • Ask them to clear nothing. You want the history intact for whatever service department sees the car next.
Write "none" if the list is empty. P0C17 on its own still counts as none.
8

Drive it, then write it down

The only check here that no scanner can do.

What to confirm on the roadDrive power under load · regen on lift-off · dash warning lamps
  • No loss of drive power under hard acceleration. That is recall 24V065, the rear driveshaft, which can break under load.
  • Regen behaves when you lift off. A dead or lurching regen points back at the charging and inverter side.
  • No warning lamp that has been cleared rather than fixed. The scan shows stored codes, the lamps show what the car thinks right now.
  • The Battery Odometer counters further down the Dashboard, charged, discharged and efficiency, are recorded by the app and may have reset with the January pack swap. No boxes, they are not worth your typing and they will not change the decision.
